# Jungian Archetypes and Symbols

## 1. The Collective Unconscious
The collective unconscious is a layer of the psyche shared among all humans, containing inherited patterns of thought and image. Unlike the personal unconscious, it is not formed by individual experience but is an innate structure.

## 2. Primary Archetypes
Archetypes are "primordial images" that shape how we perceive and interact with the world.

### The Persona
The social mask presented to the world. It is a functional necessity but can become a prison if the individual identifies entirely with the mask.

### The Shadow
The "dark side" of the personality, containing repressed desires, traumas, and traits the ego deems unacceptable. Integration of the shadow is the primary step toward wholeness.

### Anima and Animus
- **Anima:** The inner feminine element in a man's psyche.
- **Animus:** The inner masculine element in a woman's psyche.
These represent the contra-sexual aspects of the psyche that must be integrated to achieve psychological balance.

### The Self
The archetype of wholeness and the center of the entire psyche (encompassing both conscious and unconscious). The process of realizing the Self is called **Individuation**.

## 3. Symbolism and the Psyche
Symbols are different from signs. While a sign points to a specific, known meaning, a symbol is an expression of something that cannot be fully articulated in language.

- **Mandala:** A symbol of the Self and totality.
- **The Labyrinth:** Represents the journey into the unconscious and the return to the center.
- **Alchemy:** Jung viewed alchemical processes (calcination, sublimation) as metaphors for the psychological process of individuation.

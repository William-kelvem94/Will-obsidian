# Stellar Evolution and Nucleosynthesis

## Protostars and the Main Sequence
Stars form from the gravitational collapse of giant molecular clouds. Once the core temperature reaches $\approx 10^7$ K, hydrogen fusion begins via the proton-proton (pp) chain or the CNO cycle. The star reaches hydrostatic equilibrium where gravitational pressure is balanced by thermal pressure:
$$\frac{dP}{dr} = -\frac{G M(r) \rho(r)}{r^2}$$

## Advanced Burning Stages and Nucleosynthesis
Massive stars ($M > 8 M_\odot$) proceed through successive burning stages: He $\to$ C $\to$ Ne $\to$ O $\to$ Si, eventually producing an iron core. Since $^{56}\text{Fe}$ has the highest binding energy per nucleon, further fusion is endothermic, leading to core collapse.

The $s$-process (slow neutron capture) and $r$-process (rapid neutron capture) are responsible for the synthesis of elements heavier than iron. The $r$-process occurs during supernova explosions or neutron star mergers, where the neutron flux is high enough to overcome beta decay.

## Stellar Remnants
The final state depends on the remaining core mass $M_{core}$:
1. **White Dwarfs**: $M_{core} < 1.44 M_\odot$ (Chandrasekhar limit). Supported by electron degeneracy pressure.
2. **Neutron Stars**: $1.44 M_\odot < M_{core} < \approx 3 M_\odot$ (Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit). Supported by neutron degeneracy pressure.
3. **Black Holes**: $M_{core} > 3 M_\odot$. Total gravitational collapse.

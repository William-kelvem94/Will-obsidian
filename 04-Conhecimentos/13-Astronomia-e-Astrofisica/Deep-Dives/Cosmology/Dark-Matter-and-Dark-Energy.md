# Dark Matter and Dark Energy

## Dark Matter and Galactic Dynamics
The evidence for non-baryonic dark matter stems primarily from galactic rotation curves. According to Newtonian dynamics, the orbital velocity $v$ of stars at radius $r$ should follow $v(r) \propto r^{-1/2}$. Observations show a flat profile $v(r) \approx \text{constant}$, implying a dark matter halo with density $\rho(r) \propto r^{-2}$.

The mass distribution is often modeled using the Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) profile:
$$\rho(r) = \frac{\rho_0}{\frac{r}{R_s}\left(1 + \frac{r}{R_s}\right)^2}$$

## The $\Lambda$CDM Model
The $\Lambda$CDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter) model is the current concordance model. It incorporates a cosmological constant $\Lambda$ to account for dark energy and cold (non-relativistic) dark matter. The energy budget is approximately $\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.68$, $\Omega_{cdm} \approx 0.27$, and $\Omega_{b} \approx 0.05$.

## Dark Energy and Cosmic Acceleration
Observations of Type Ia Supernovae reveal that the expansion of the universe is accelerating. This is modeled by the equation of state $w = P/\rho$. For a cosmological constant, $w = -1$, leading to a constant energy density as the universe expands.

The acceleration is given by the second Friedmann equation:
$$\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho + \frac{3P}{c^2}\right)$$
Acceleration $\ddot{a} > 0$ occurs when $P < -\frac{1}{3}\rho c^2$, characterizing the repulsive nature of dark energy.

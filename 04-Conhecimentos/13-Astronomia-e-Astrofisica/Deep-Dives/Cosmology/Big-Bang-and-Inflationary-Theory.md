# Big Bang and Inflationary Theory

## The Initial Singularity and General Relativity
The standard cosmological model posits that the universe originated from a gravitational singularity, a point of infinite density and temperature where the laws of General Relativity break down. The evolution of the scale factor $a(t)$ is governed by the Friedmann equations:

$$H^2 = \left( \frac{\dot{a}}{a} \right)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{kc^2}{a^2}$$

where $H$ is the Hubble parameter, $\rho$ is the energy density, and $k$ is the curvature constant.

## Cosmic Inflation
To resolve the horizon and flatness problems, the inflationary paradigm proposes an era of exponential expansion between $10^{-36}$ and $10^{-32}$ seconds. This is driven by a scalar field $\phi$, the inflaton, with a potential $V(\phi)$. The condition for inflation is the slow-roll approximation:

$$\epsilon = \frac{M_{pl}^2}{2} \left( \frac{V'}{V} \right)^2 \ll 1, \quad \eta = M_{pl}^2 \frac{V''}{V} \ll 1$$

This expansion smoothed out the curvature of the universe and pushed any initial inhomogeneities beyond the observable horizon.

## Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)
The CMB is the relic radiation from the epoch of recombination ($z \approx 1100$), when electrons and protons formed neutral hydrogen, allowing photons to decouple. The power spectrum of CMB temperature fluctuations $\Delta T/T$ provides critical data on the baryon density $\Omega_b$ and cold dark matter density $\Omega_{cdm}$.

The angular power spectrum is characterized by acoustic peaks, where the first peak position determines the geometry of the universe:
$$\theta_{peak} \approx \frac{r_s(z_{rec})}{D_A(z_{rec})}$$

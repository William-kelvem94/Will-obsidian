# Cognitive Psychology Architecture: A Comprehensive Treatise on Mental Processing

## I. The Architecture of Human Perception

Perception is not a passive registration of external stimuli but an active construction of reality. The process begins with **transduction**, where physical energy (light, sound waves) is converted into electrochemical signals.

### 1. Bottom-Up vs. Top-Down Processing
- **Bottom-Up Processing (Data-Driven):** The analysis begins with the sensory input. For instance, the retina detects edges and contrasts before the brain identifies an object.
- **Top-Down Processing (Concept-Driven):** The application of prior knowledge, expectations, and context to interpret sensory data. This explains the phenomenon of *perceptual set*, where a subject is predisposed to perceive a stimulus in a particular way.

### 2. Gestalt Principles of Organization
The human mind organizes visual elements into coherent wholes rather than fragmented parts:
- **Proximity:** Elements close to each other are perceived as a group.
- **Similarity:** Similar items are grouped together.
- **Continuity:** The eye follows the smoothest path.
- **Closure:** The mind fills in gaps to create a complete object.

## II. Memory Systems: The Temporal Continuum of Information

Memory is not a unitary faculty but a distributed system of encoding, storage, and retrieval.

### 1. Sensory Memory
The most fleeting stage of memory.
- **Iconic Memory:** Visual sensory store (lasting ~0.5 seconds).
- **Echoic Memory:** Auditory sensory store (lasting 3-4 seconds).

### 2. Short-Term and Working Memory
Working memory is the active "workbench" of the mind. According to the **Baddeley-Hitch model**, it consists of:
- **Central Executive:** The attentional control system.
- **Phonological Loop:** Processes auditory information.
- **Visuospatial Sketchpad:** Processes visual and spatial information.
- **Episodic Buffer:** Integrates information across buffers into a single chronological sequence.

### 3. Long-Term Memory (LTM)
LTM has virtually unlimited capacity and persists indefinitely.
- **Declarative (Explicit) Memory:**
  - *Episodic:* Personal experiences and events.
  - *Semantic:* General knowledge and facts.
- **Non-Declarative (Implicit) Memory:**
  - *Procedural:* Motor skills and habits (e.g., riding a bicycle).
  - *Priming:* Enhanced identification of stimuli based on previous exposure.

## III. Cognitive Biases and Heuristics

The brain employs heuristics—mental shortcuts—to reduce cognitive load, which often results in systematic errors known as cognitive biases.

### 1. Availability Heuristic
The tendency to overestimate the importance of information that is most readily available (e.s., news reports of plane crashes vs. car accidents).

### 2. Confirmation Bias
The inclination to search for, interpret, and remember information that confirms one's pre-existing beliefs, while disregarding contradictory evidence.

### 3. Anchoring Effect
The tendency to rely too heavily on the first piece of information offered (the "anchor") when making decisions.

## IV. Synthesis and Interconnectivity

The architecture of cognition is a recursive loop where [[Neuropsychology-of-Learning|synaptic plasticity]] enables the transition from working memory to long-term storage. These systems are further modulated by the behavioral reinforcements detailed in [[Behavioral-Analysis-and-Conditioning]].
